# IP-SAKTI — IP RAG Mapping

## Purpose

This document maps common Intellectual Property questions to the
authoritative documents and legal topics that should be retrieved by
the IP-SAKTI RAG system.

The system should prioritize Indian primary legal sources for questions
about Indian law.

# 2. Traditional Knowledge and Patentability

## Example Question

Can an invention based on traditionally known medicinal knowledge
be patented?

## Retrieve

- DOC-IP-001 — The Patents Act, 1970
- DOC-IP-003 — Guidelines for Processing of Patent Applications relating to Traditional Knowledge and Biological Material - 2012
- DOC-IP-004 — Guidelines for Examination of Ayush Related Inventions-2025
- DOC-IP-011 — WIPO Key Questions on Patent Disclosure Requirements for Genetic Resources and Traditional Knowledge

## Important Topic

### Section 3(p)

Check whether the claimed invention is:

- an invention which is in effect traditional knowledge; or
- an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally
  known components.

The system should examine the facts and retrieved evidence before
giving an answer.

# 5. Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL)

## Example Question

Why is TKDL relevant to patent examination?

## Retrieve

- DOC-IP-003 — Guidelines for Processing of Patent Applications relating to Traditional Knowledge and Biological Material - 2012
- DOC-IP-004 — Guidelines for Examination of Ayush Related Inventions-2025
- DOC-IP-013 — Intellectual Property and Genetic Resources, Traditional Knowledge and Traditional Cultural Expressions - An Overview

## Important Topics

- Traditional knowledge as prior-art information
- Search and examination
- Codified traditional knowledge
- Ayurveda and other Indian systems of medicine

## Important Caution

The system must not state that the TKDL database itself is the
underlying prior art.

The underlying traditional knowledge sources are relevant prior-art
materials. TKDL is a tool/database that helps patent offices identify
such knowledge.

The system must not claim that the full TKDL database is publicly
available for unrestricted copying or scraping.

# 15. Genetic Resources and Traditional Knowledge

## Example Question

What intellectual-property issues can arise when genetic resources

## Retrieve

- DOC-IP-011 — WIPO Key Questions on Patent Disclosure Requirements for Genetic Resources and Traditional Knowledge - Second Edition
- DOC-IP-013 — Intellectual Property and Genetic Resources, Traditional Knowledge and Traditional Cultural Expressions - An Overview
- DOC-IP-012 — WIPO Treaty on Intellectual Property, Genetic Resources and Associated Traditional Knowledge

## Important Topics

- Genetic resources
- Traditional knowledge

## Legal Status Warning

WIPO materials are international/reference materials.

They must not automatically be presented as Indian domestic law.

The WIPO GRATK Treaty should be described as an international treaty
and not as an Indian statutory requirement unless applicable Indian law
or another authoritative source establishes that requirement.

# 16. Source Priority

When multiple sources are retrieved, use the following priority order
for questions concerning Indian law:

1. The Patents Act, 1970
2. The Patents Rules, 2003
3. Relevant official IP India guidelines
4. Official IP India manuals
5. WIPO reference materials
6. Other permitted authoritative sources

Primary Indian legal sources should take priority over international
reference material when answering questions about Indian law.

---

# 17. Citation Requirements

Every legal or patentability answer should identify the supporting
source.

Where possible, the system should provide:

- Document title
- Relevant section/rule/topic
- Source authority
- Citation or document reference

The answer should not rely on unsupported claims.

---

# 18. Legal Answering Rules

The system should:

- Distinguish Indian law from international reference material.
- Distinguish Acts, Rules, Guidelines and Manuals.
- Retrieve evidence before answering.
- Cite the relevant evidence.
- Avoid unsupported legal conclusions.
- Avoid claiming that an invention is definitely patentable or
  definitely unpatentable without sufficient facts.
- Clearly state when additional facts or professional legal advice may
  be required.
- Prefer the most authoritative and applicable source.

# 19. High-Priority IP-SAKTI Query

Example:

"I developed an Ayurvedic formulation using a traditionally known
medicinal plant. Can I patent it in India, and could biodiversity or
traditional-knowledge requirements apply?"

## Retrieve

### IP Sources

- DOC-IP-001 — The Patents Act, 1970
- DOC-IP-003 — Guidelines for Processing of Patent Applications relating to Traditional Knowledge and Biological Material - 2012
- DOC-IP-004 — Guidelines for Examination of Ayush Related Inventions-2025
- DOC-IP-005 — Manual of Patent Office Practice and Procedure - 2019

### Supporting International Sources

- DOC-IP-011 — WIPO Key Questions on Patent Disclosure Requirements for Genetic Resources and Traditional Knowledge
- DOC-IP-013 — WIPO Intellectual Property and Genetic Resources, Traditional Knowledge and Traditional Cultural Expressions Overview

## Check

1. Whether the subject matter qualifies as an invention.
2. Novelty.
3. Inventive step.
4. Industrial applicability.
5. Section 3 exclusions.
6. Whether the claimed subject matter is based on traditional
   knowledge.
7. Whether Section 3(p) is relevant.
8. Whether biological material or genetic resources are involved.
9. Whether additional biodiversity/TK requirements need to be
   considered under applicable Indian law.
10. Provide citations for the conclusions.

## Expected Response Structure

### Short Answer

Give a concise answer based on the available evidence.

### Patentability

Explain the relevant patentability requirements.

### Traditional Knowledge

Explain whether traditional knowledge may affect the analysis.

### Biological Material / Biodiversity

Explain whether biological material or biodiversity-related
requirements may need to be investigated.

### Evidence

List the relevant official sources and sections.

### Limitation

Do not give a definitive legal conclusion when the available facts are
insufficient.
