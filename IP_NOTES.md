# IP Knowledge Notes

## 1. Patents in India

### 1.1 Core Patent Law

The primary Indian law governing patents is the Patents Act, 1970.

Supporting procedural rules are provided by the Patents Rules, 2003.

### 1.2 Patentability

When evaluating whether an invention may be patentable in India, the system should check the applicable legal requirements, including:

- Novelty
- Inventive step
- Industrial applicability
- Whether the subject matter qualifies as an invention
- Whether the subject matter is excluded from patent protection

### 1.3 Traditional Knowledge

Traditional knowledge is particularly important for inventions involving Ayurveda and other traditional systems of medicine.

The system should check whether the claimed invention is based on existing traditional knowledge before concluding that it is patentable.

### 1.4 Biological Material

If an invention involves biological material, the system should identify the relevant patent requirements and applicable guidelines.

### 1.5 AYUSH Inventions

AYUSH-related inventions are examined under the applicable patentability requirements.

For an Ayurvedic invention, the system should consider:

- Novelty
- Inventive step
- Industrial applicability
- Excluded subject matter
- Traditional knowledge
- Biological material
- Applicable disclosure and procedural requirements

## 2. Trade Marks

Trade marks provide protection for signs such as brand names, logos and other marks used in relation to goods or services.

The main Indian legislation is the Trade Marks Act, 1999, supported by the Trade Marks Rules, 2017.

## 3. Geographical Indications

Geographical indications protect goods associated with a particular geographical origin and possessing qualities, reputation or characteristics attributable to that origin.

The main Indian legislation is the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999, supported by the GI Rules.

## 4. Genetic Resources and Traditional Knowledge

Genetic resources and traditional knowledge can create additional IP and disclosure considerations.

The system should distinguish:

- Indian domestic law
- Indian administrative guidelines
- International WIPO reference material
- International treaty material

International WIPO material should not automatically be treated as Indian domestic law.

## 5. IP-SAKTI Demo Scenario

Example:

"I developed an Ayurvedic formulation using a traditionally known medicinal plant. Can I patent it in India, and could biodiversity or traditional-knowledge requirements apply?"

The system should identify the relevant issues before producing an answer:

1. Patentability
2. Traditional knowledge
3. Biological material
4. Genetic resources
5. Biodiversity-related requirements
6. AYUSH-related examination

The final response should be supported by authoritative sources and citations.

## 8. Key Patents Act Sections

### Section 2(1)(ac) — Industrial Application

An invention must be capable of being made or used in an industry.

### Section 2(1)(j) — Invention

An invention means a new product or process involving an inventive step and capable of industrial application.

### Section 2(1)(ja) — Inventive Step

An inventive step involves technical advance compared with existing knowledge or economic significance or both, and makes the invention not obvious to a person skilled in the art.

### Section 2(1)(l) — New Invention

A new invention is one that has not been anticipated by publication or use before the relevant filing/priority date and has not fallen into the public domain or become part of the state of the art.

### Section 3 — What Are Not Inventions

Section 3 identifies subject matter that is not considered an invention for purposes of the Patents Act.

For IP-SAKTI, relevant exclusions include:

- Section 3(e): Certain mere admixtures where the result is only an aggregation of properties of the components.
- Section 3(i): Certain methods of treatment of humans or animals.
- Section 3(j): Plants, animals and essentially biological processes, subject to the statutory exceptions.
- Section 3(p): An invention which, in effect, is traditional knowledge, or which is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known components.

### Section 3(p) — Traditional Knowledge

Section 3(p) is particularly important for IP-SAKTI.

An invention that is, in effect, traditional knowledge, or an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known components, is excluded from patentability.

Therefore, when a user asks whether an Ayurvedic formulation based on traditional knowledge can be patented, the system should specifically investigate Section 3(p).

### Section 8 — Information Regarding Foreign Applications

Patent applicants may have obligations concerning information and undertakings relating to applications for patents outside India.

The system should check the applicable requirements when a user asks about corresponding foreign patent applications.

### Section 10 — Contents of Specifications

Patent specifications must satisfy the applicable requirements concerning the description and disclosure of the invention.

The system should consider Section 10 when questions concern what must be disclosed in a patent specification.

### Section 13 — Search for Anticipation

Patent examination includes consideration of whether the invention has been anticipated by earlier publication or prior claims.

This is relevant to novelty and prior-art assessment.

### Section 25 — Opposition

The Patents Act provides grounds and procedures for opposition to the grant of a patent.

The system should distinguish opposition questions from initial patentability questions.

## 9. High-Priority IP-SAKTI Rule

For an Ayurvedic invention involving a traditionally known medicinal plant, the system should not immediately conclude that the invention is patentable.

It should investigate:

1. Whether the claimed subject matter is new.
2. Whether it involves an inventive step.
3. Whether it has industrial applicability.
4. Whether any Section 3 exclusion applies.
5. Whether Section 3(p) is relevant because of traditional knowledge.
6. Whether biological material or genetic resources are involved.
7. Whether additional biodiversity-related requirements may apply.
8. Which authoritative source supports each conclusion.

### Source Priority

For Indian patent-law questions, the system should prioritize:

1. Patents Act, 1970
2. Patents Rules, 2003
3. Relevant IP India guidelines
4. Patent Office Manual
5. WIPO materials as international/reference material

The system should not treat WIPO treaty or reference material as automatically equivalent to Indian domestic law.
